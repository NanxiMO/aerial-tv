Aerial

A calm home screen for Android TV. No ads, no recommendation feeds. Just your apps, your wallpaper, and Apple TV's aerial screensavers.

Quiet is
the best interface.

Turn on the TV and you see the time and the apps you actually use.

Home

Every app,
at its best.

Apps with a TV banner show it. Square icons fill the tile with their own color. A white ring marks the app in focus, so you can spot it from the couch.

System apps without a launcher entry, like Xiaomi's App Store and Mi Home, show up too.

Organize

Hold OK
to make it yours.

Move, hide, inspect or uninstall any app. Tiles make room as you move one, and hidden apps are always a click away in Control Center.

Control Center

Everyday controls,
one button away.

Inputs, network and settings, plus volume and brightness. Network and settings open your TV's own panels, no menu diving. Pin an HDMI input to Home to switch to it in one press.

When you're not watching,
enjoy the view.

114 aerial films from Apple TV, from orbit to the ocean floor, with location names as the camera moves.

Wallpaper

A new view every day,
or your own photos.

Bing's photo of the day, eight built-in gradients, or pictures from your phone.

Set up from your phone

Scan.
Don't type.

Scan the code on the TV to upload photos, pick a wallpaper or change the screensaver. The page is only open while the wallpaper panel is, behind a random token.

Home at boot.
Still home after standby.

Most TVs sold in China lock you into the maker's launcher and kill other apps in standby. Aerial works around both, with no root and without disabling a single system app.

Owns the Home button

Aerial becomes the default home screen where it can. Where the firmware won't allow it, Home Guard brings Aerial back whenever the stock launcher appears. The TV's own launcher is still one tile away.

Back in half a second

Xiaomi TVs force-stop other apps in standby. When Android reconnects Aerial's notification listener, Aerial comes right back.

Survives a power cut

Aerial takes over at boot. TCL blocks third-party apps from starting at boot; the installer lets Aerial through.

Turn on ADB debugging

      
The first time your computer connects, the TV asks whether to allow USB debugging. Choose Allow and check Always allow. Chinese menu names are in brackets.

      

        
Xiaomi / RedmiSettings (设置) → About (关于) → press OK on Model (产品型号) until it says developer mode is on. Then Settings → Account & Security (账号与安全): turn on ADB debugging (ADB 调试) and Install unknown apps (安装未知来源的应用).

        
TCL / FFALCONSettings (设置) → About (关于) → Device info (本机信息), then press Up, Down, Left, Right on the remote, less than a second apart. A More (更多) group appears at the bottom; turn on ADB there.

        
Android TV, Google TV, Hisense and othersSettings → Device Preferences → About → press Build 7 times. Then turn on USB debugging in Developer options (sometimes called Network debugging or ADB debugging).

      

    

  






  

    
FAQ

    

      

        Can I install it straight from a web page?
        
No. Browsers can't talk to the TV's ADB port, and a TV's USB port can't act as a device for your computer. The agent prompt and the one-line command are as close as it gets.

      

      

        Can it break my TV? How do I undo it?
        
Aerial never uninstalls, disables or changes system apps. It grants itself a few permissions and changes the home screen and screensaver settings. Replace the final bash in the one-line command with bash -s restore to revert everything; add --uninstall to remove Aerial as well.

      

      

        What permissions does it use?
        
WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS turns Home Guard back on if the system switches it off. The notification listener lets Android restart Aerial after it's killed or the TV boots; it reads no notifications. The accessibility service only sees which window is in front, never what's on screen. Location is only used to show your Wi-Fi network's name.

      

      

        Where do the aerial films come from? How much data do they use?
        
They stream straight from Apple's servers; Aerial only stores their addresses and location names. Expect 1080p H.264 at about 15–20 Mbps. If they can't load, Aerial switches to the photo screensaver.

      

      

        My TV isn't listed. Will it work?
        
Probably. Stock Android TV and Google TV are the easiest. If something goes wrong, open an issue on GitHub with your TV model and the installer's output.
